AND THE LEGAL EXPOSURE IS RISING TOO

This isn't just a market you're missing. It's a filing you're exposed to.

It usually isn't a customer who reports a broken site — plaintiffs' firms run automated scanners across thousands of sites at once, looking for the same handful of failures. Federal lawsuits are only the visible part; for every one filed, several more businesses get a demand letter instead and quietly settle.

Both cost real money, and neither is optional to respond to. A lawsuit typically settles for $5,000 to $75,000 before your own legal fees — and you still have to fix the site afterward. A demand letter rarely reaches a courtroom, but ignoring one isn't free: you're paying an attorney to respond either way, and most still end in a negotiated settlement. The letter is cheaper to make go away than the lawsuit it turns into if you don't.

WHY THIS ISN'T AN AI JOB

A scanner can't sit where a screen reader user sits.

Automated tools are real and useful — they catch missing alt text and contrast ratios fast. But industry research puts their overall coverage at roughly 30–40% of WCAG issues. The rest requires a person, and the gap is worst in exactly the two tests that matter most: whether you can operate a site by keyboard, and whether a screen reader actually makes sense of it.

Color contrast 98%
Overall WCAG coverage 30–40%
Screen reader compatibility ~23%
Keyboard navigation ~2%

Automated coverage share, by category — what a scanner can reliably catch on its own.

Keyboard navigation and screen reader compatibility are the two things I test by hand on every audit, because they're the two things a scanner is worst at. That's not a sales line — it's the reason a $1,500 audit and a free automated scan aren't the same product.

Sources: Deque Automated Accessibility Coverage Report; UK Government Digital Service tool comparison; Accessibility.Works research, 2026.

Checkout field has no accessible name

Filter controls don't register

Sort and filter options are visually present but never announce as selectable — the feature doesn't work for a screen reader user at all.

FIX: pair each input with one matching <label for>

Contrast below the required ratio

Body text at 3.1:1 against its background — under the 4.5:1 minimum. Hard to read for a lot of visitors, not only screen reader users.
